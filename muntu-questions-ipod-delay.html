Muntu Questions IPOD Delay as Three Opposition Parties Await Admission

24 November 2026 · SSAZA FM 99.8

Alliance for National Transformation (ANT) founding member Maj. Gen. (Rtd) Mugisha Muntu has questioned delays in admitting three opposition parties into the Inter-Party Organisation for Dialogue (IPOD), saying the failure to convene the organisation’s summit has stalled their participation.

Muntu said ANT, the National Unity Platform (NUP) and the People’s Front for Freedom (PFF) had applied to join IPOD after securing representation in Parliament but were still awaiting formal approval.

He explained that under IPOD’s current memorandum of understanding, parties seeking admission must submit applications for consideration and approval by the organisation’s summit. However, he said the summit had not convened to conclude the process, leaving the three parties waiting.

“We all applied. Three parties ourselves, we applied. NUP applied. PFF applied. We are not members in the last term. So the summit was supposed to sit and accept the three. The summit has never met. So we keep waiting, we keep watching,” Muntu said.

He said the delay had persisted for about four to five months since Parliament was sworn in, questioning why the admission process had not been completed despite the parties expressing willingness to join.

Change in admission requirements

Muntu argued that the current membership arrangement had introduced additional bureaucratic requirements that were absent under the previous system. He said a political party previously qualified to participate in IPOD automatically after securing representation in Parliament.

However, he said the revised memorandum of understanding requires parties seeking admission to formally apply before the summit can approve their membership.

“The moment you get a member of Parliament, automatically you become a member of IPOD and become operational. But then, in the last five years, the members of IPOD sat and wrote a briefing in the memorandum of understanding that any party which goes into Parliament first has to apply,” he said.

Muntu said the requirement for summit approval had effectively stalled the process because the meeting responsible for considering the applications had not taken place.

Questions over convening the summit

He also questioned the availability of the ruling National Resistance Movement (NRM) leadership to facilitate the process, saying the party’s leader had not been available since Parliament was sworn in.

He suggested that the NRM leadership should either convene the required meeting or delegate representatives to ensure that the outstanding applications are considered.

Although Muntu expressed uncertainty about which political party currently holds the responsibility of chairing IPOD, he maintained that the failure to convene the summit had affected the admission process.

Funding and political dialogue

The membership dispute has taken on added significance following changes to the legal framework governing political parties, which link participation in IPOD to access to government funding.

In July 2026, reports indicated that IPOD had resolved to admit ANT and NUP. However, subsequent reports in August suggested that the parties were still going through the formal admission process.

The organisation’s governing council also recommended PFF’s admission in September, with formal confirmation expected at a subsequent summit.

Muntu said he hoped the outstanding administrative issues would be resolved to allow the three opposition parties to participate in IPOD’s activities.

The delay has also raised questions about the organisation’s capacity to facilitate dialogue among political parties at a time when relations between the government and opposition remain strained.

IPOD was established to provide a platform for political parties to engage on issues of national importance, promote dialogue and address disagreements through peaceful political engagement.

Muntu said the immediate priority was to complete the admission process and allow parties that had expressed interest and fulfilled the stated requirements to participate in the organisation’s activities. He said the opposition parties would continue waiting for the summit to resolve the matter.
